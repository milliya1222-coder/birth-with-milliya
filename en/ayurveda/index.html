For Whom

This treatment exists for you

This tradition comes from Kerala in southern India. It was designed first for mothers after birth — but it belongs equally to any woman who wants to come back into her own body.

Mothers within six months of birth

Vaginal or caesarean birth alike, and especially within the first 42 days. Supports the uterus in returning, settles postpartum anxiety, and helps with sleep and the physical toll of breastfeeding.

Women with gynaecological concerns

Irregular or painful periods, PMS, poor pelvic circulation, chronic fatigue, hormonal disruption from long-term stress — all of these respond to sustained Ayurvedic care.

Women running on empty

Persistent anxiety, insomnia, a mind that will not stop, the sense that your body is not quite yours. Ayurveda calls this excess Vāta, and warm-oil massage is its most direct answer.

Women preparing to conceive

For those who want to nourish the reproductive system and settle the nervous system before pregnancy — meeting a new life from a well-resourced body.

Why Ayurveda

An answer with five thousand years behind it

Āyurveda — literally "the knowledge of life" — reads a woman's body and mind as one system that cycles and remembers. Menstruation, birth, menopause, sustained stress: each leaves a physical imprint. Left unattended, those imprints accumulate as insomnia, anxiety, chronic fatigue and gynaecological difficulty.

The six weeks after birth are called Sutika Kala — the window in which a mother's body and emotions recover. But even outside that window, Vāta — the energy governing movement, change and nerve conduction — goes into excess very easily in women. Modern pace, overthinking and postpartum hormonal shifts all push it out of balance.

Excess Vāta = insomnia, anxiety, bloating, stiff joints, a mind that will not settle, the feeling that your body is not your own.
And the most direct answer to Vāta is warm oil — worked deep enough into the fascia that the body finally lets go.

Three moments — the gaze, the oil, and being caught

The Kerala tradition brings together three strands — massage, herbal steam and herbal bath — toward one end: that you come back to living inside your own body.

What

What the full therapy includes

The introductory session is massage only (Abhyanga). The full therapy adds herbal steam and herbal bath — together they do considerably more than any one of them alone.

From the palms into the fascia

Abhyanga is not surface touch. Warm oil and hands are absorbed together. When your eyes close and the muscles hand themselves over one by one, that is your body saying: at last, someone has taken this from me.

01 Warm-oil full body massage Abhyanga Core treatment

Traditionally prepared herb-infused oil — not essential oil — warmed and worked from head to foot. It settles a dysregulated nervous system directly. This is the heart of the therapy and the whole of the introductory session.

Safety

When to wait, and when to ask first

Please postpone or speak with me first if any of these apply — I assess this fully before we book

  • Postpartum bleeding has not stopped, lochia is abnormal, or there are signs of infection
  • A caesarean incision has not yet healed (I suggest waiting three weeks before Abhyanga)
  • During menstruation (steam and herbal bath are best avoided)
  • Acute fever, significant illness, skin infection or open wounds
  • A known allergy to particular herbs or oils
  • You are taking anticoagulant medication
  • You have been diagnosed with severe postpartum depression and are under psychiatric care — treatment should lead; this can support alongside it
  • You are undergoing cancer treatment (please discuss with your doctor first)
FAQ

Things you might want to ask

How soon after birth can I start?
After a vaginal birth, from around 7–10 days. After a caesarean, from three weeks, once the incision is stable. I confirm the right starting point with you in the consultation before we book. Any time within six months is welcome, and earlier is better.
Can I come if I am not postpartum?
Yes. What this work actually does is settle Vāta and nourish a woman's system. Postpartum is where it is needed most acutely, but it suits any woman wanting deep restoration, help with gynaecological difficulty, or release from long-held stress. Women preparing to conceive are very welcome.
Studio or home visit?
Either. The Taipei studio is in Beitou, with full equipment and a quiet room; the Zushi salon is in Kanagawa. Home visits suit mothers after birth or anyone for whom leaving the house is difficult, with travel charged by distance.
Can I have this while breastfeeding? Will the herbs affect my milk?
Yes, and Abhyanga helps circulation and emotional steadiness for breastfeeding mothers. The herbal oils are external and do not enter breast milk. The herbs used in yoni steam are selected carefully — please tell me at consultation if you are sensitive to particular plants.
Can my family be home? What about the baby?
Family at home is completely fine. I suggest arranging for someone to hold the baby so you can genuinely let go during the session. If you are on your own with your baby, we adjust the pace and pause whenever needed.
How is this different from a spa massage?
A spa addresses relaxation; this addresses repair. Three differences: the oils and technique are a traditional protocol for excess Vāta; I come to your body as a doula and integration coach who understands what you have just been through, so it is never only the body being worked on; and this is a system with five thousand years of theory behind it, not a one-off treatment.
How do I choose between massage only and the full therapy?
If Ayurveda is new to you, begin with the introductory session and get to know your body and this way of working. If you are postpartum, have a clear gynaecological concern, or want deep restoration, go straight to the full therapy with steam and herbal bath.
What if I feel unwell partway through?
You can stop at any moment and the pace follows you. I check in on how your body is doing before and after every session, and if anything is wrong I adjust immediately or refer you on.

About

Who will be with you

I am Milliya — a birth doula and an integration coach for women's body, mind and spirit.

Across the births I have attended, I have come to think the real test of having a baby is not the hours of labour. It is the forty-two days afterwards, when nobody actually asks how you are. And it is not only postpartum — through menstruation, through trying to conceive, through years of accumulated stress, a great many women are living in that same state of not being properly held.

I went to Ayurveda because it gave me language. It connects something as vague as "my body feels hollow" to a system with five thousand years of accumulated observation behind it. It is not another kind of spa. It answers why this is happening, and it tells you what to do.

I bring together the doula's view, training in women's body-mind-spirit integration, and the Ayurvedic postpartum massage practicum I completed at Ganesha Space in Hualien in May 2026 — and I have brought that back to Taipei, and to Zushi.

Not because I can care for you better than a postpartum centre can. Because women in this era need more than one way of being caught.
