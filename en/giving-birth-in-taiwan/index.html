Giving birth in Taiwan
as a foreigner

This is a research snapshot, not a live directory. Everything here was checked in August 2026 and graded by how solid the evidence was. Clinics close, doctors move, programmes end — some of what follows will be out of date before you read it. Use it to know what questions to ask, then confirm each detail yourself before you rely on it.

Milliya Researched August 2026 About the author →

If you are pregnant in Taiwan and you don't read Chinese, you have probably already found the same handful of blog posts everyone else finds. Some are excellent. Most are several years old, and a few repeat things that are no longer true.

So we did the research properly — five parallel investigations across official sources, hospital websites, academic literature, government statistics and expat communities — and then graded every finding by how well we could actually verify it.

Start here: are you actually insured?

This is the single most consequential thing to get straight, and it catches people out constantly. National Health Insurance eligibility depends on how you hold your residency, not on how long you've been in the country.

Your situationWhen you can enrol
Employed (including internships and training programmes)No waiting period. Your employer enrols you from your first day of work.
Not employed — residency through a spouse, investment, or other groundsAfter six months of residence in Taiwan.

The six-month rule counts continuous residence, or residence with a single absence of no more than 30 days, with the days abroad deducted. Confirmed against the NHI Administration website, August 2026.

Once you are insured, prenatal care and birth are covered — and under Article 48 of the National Health Insurance Act, childbirth itself is exempt from copayment. What you actually pay for are the optional extras: epidural (roughly NT$8,000–10,000), a private room (around NT$3,000 per night), and an elective caesarean without medical indication (roughly NT$20,000–30,000 more).

Without insurance, expect to pay in full. One reported vaginal birth with epidural and two nights came to NT$43,776, though that figure is several years old. We could not find an official schedule for uninsured caesarean costs — Unverified. Ask the hospital's international desk for a quote rather than trusting any number you read online, including ours.

What prenatal care looks like

Since July 2021, publicly funded prenatal visits increased from 10 to 14, adding gestational diabetes screening, anaemia testing and two additional standard ultrasounds (three in total). Confirmed — Ministry of Health and Welfare.

Eligibility follows your insurance, not your nationality. If you're enrolled in NHI, you get the same subsidised schedule as everyone else.

At your first visit you'll receive a pregnancy handbook. There are English, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Thai and Khmer editions — but note the English edition dates from 2022 while the Chinese version has since been revised. Use both if you can.

One thing Japanese and Western patients both mention

Taiwanese doctors typically tell you only your next appointment date, rather than handing you a full schedule of the whole pregnancy. If you're used to a printed timeline, this can feel unsettling. It isn't neglect — it's just a different convention. You can ask for the full schedule; most clinics will walk you through it if you ask directly.

Hospitals with international services

Read this before the list

In our research, only one hospital explicitly listed obstetrics and gynaecology within its international medical service scope on its own website: China Medical University Hospital in Taichung.

Every other institution below could only be confirmed as having an international desk and English pages. That is not the same thing. Most international medical centres in Taiwan are built around medical tourism, health screening and complex referrals — routine prenatal care runs through standard NHI clinics, which may not be staffed in English.

So: call the international desk, and ask specifically about obstetrics. Ask whether English support covers prenatal visits, labour, and the postpartum ward — they are often different answers.

If you want a low-intervention birth

Correction: a programme you may have read about no longer exists

Discontinued Several older articles refer to Taiwan's "friendly and diverse gentle birth" pilot programme and name six participating hospitals. That programme launched in October 2014, ran for roughly six months, and was not continued. By the time it ended only one hospital was still participating. Reporting also indicates some participating hospitals never actually employed midwives to attend births.

If you take that six-hospital list to a registration desk today, you will be disappointed. Please don't.

Here is what we could actually verify as of August 2026.

Taipei City Hospital, Heping Fuyou BranchDesk only

Designated a maternal-child specialist hospital under Taipei City policy, with a physician–midwife collaborative care model. In April 2025 it completed its first family-centred waterbirth — the mother's husband and two young children were present throughout, and her eldest child cut the cord.

Note this was a first, not a routine service. Call to ask whether waterbirth is still offered and what conditions apply.

02-2391-6470 · 12 Fuzhou St, Taipei

Taichung Tzu Chi HospitalDesk only

A stated gentle-birth collaborative care model: birth plans, shared decision-making, delayed cord clamping, perineal massage and pelvic relaxation, partner involvement. Since March 2026, a midwife consultation clinic runs on alternate Saturdays alongside the physician's prenatal clinic. This is the most recent development we found.

Dianthus Minquan branchDesk only

LDR rooms, individualised birth plans, and a dedicated birth pool (around 60cm deep), with waterbirth consultation offered at 32–36 weeks. Their site features a Russian mother's waterbirth supported throughout by an English-speaking nurse. Fees and eligibility criteria are not published.

Waterbirth

We could verify only two places in Taiwan with documented waterbirth: the two above. There is no official or professional-association register of waterbirth providers. The many Chinese-language articles introducing waterbirth are health-education or marketing content and do not indicate that the publisher offers the service.

Midwifery clinics and home birth

Midwife-attended births account for under 1% of all births in Taiwan. Roughly 300–500 midwives are in active practice, trained at just two institutions.

As of May 2024 there were 16 NHI-contracted midwifery clinics nationwide, listed by the National Union of Midwives Associations. Most are small, individually run practices. Community Because that list is over a year old and several practitioners are approaching retirement, we would not reproduce it here without calling each one first — which we have not done.

The clearest publicly advertised home-birth service we found is Good Pregnancy Midwifery Clinic (好孕助產所) in Da'an, Taipei, serving greater Taipei, Yilan, and parts of the south. They offer birth at home, at the clinic, or with a midwife accompanying you into a partner hospital. An in-person consultation is required first.

One practical barrier for foreign families: most midwifery teams conduct care and antenatal classes in Mandarin, and some require that parents can communicate in Chinese. This is, in effect, how many foreign women are excluded from the low-intervention option entirely.

Doula and language support

We will be blunt, because the alternative is misleading you.

Publicly findable English-language doula support in Taiwan is, essentially, one person. Across three independent sources spanning 2018, 2022 and 2023, the same name appears, and more than one describes her as the only English-speaking doula many people in Taipei know of. She is Canadian, has lived in Taiwan over 25 years, has practised for 15+ years, and works in English and fluent Mandarin.

Community Neither DONA International nor the HypnoBirthing Institute lists any practitioner in Taiwan in their public directories. If you are used to checking a certification register before hiring, Taiwan will look to you like a country where this profession does not exist. It does — it is simply not registered in the places you would look.

We also found no publicly available English-language HypnoBirthing, Lamaze or Bradley Method course in Taiwan.

What the gap actually is

Reporting in the Taipei Times identifies the core problems as overuse of episiotomy and caesarean section, insufficient informed consent, and staff declining to speak English.

Those are not language problems. They are power problems. A translation app cannot help you in the moment the scissors are already in someone's hand.

What is missing in Taiwan is not information. It is someone in the room who speaks your language and is willing to speak up on your behalf.

Postpartum: the month you didn't plan for

Taiwan's postpartum care centre (產後護理之家, colloquially 月子中心) is a licensed nursing institution regulated by local health authorities. It is not covered by NHI. Many families move in directly from the hospital for around a month.

Typical rates (2024–2025 industry figures, not official): northern Taiwan roughly NT$4,800–8,000 per night, central NT$3,500–7,000, southern NT$3,000–6,500, premium NT$8,000–12,000+. A 30-day stay commonly lands between NT$90,000 and NT$420,000. Expat community reports in Taipei range from NT$120,000 to NT$800,000. Book at five to six months of pregnancy — the good ones fill up.

Desk only Dianthus postpartum centres (Shilin and Taoyuan) are the only ones we found stating English and Japanese service in writing on an English site, from around NT$10,900 per night. Community Gemcare (人之初) in Taipei appears most often in English media, but its own website is Chinese-only.

For the many other well-known chains, we found no English pages and no expat reports, so we are not listing them.

After the birth: paperwork

If one parent is a Taiwanese national, your child has Taiwanese nationality and you do household registration at a household registration office within 60 days.

If both parents are foreign nationals, the path is different and shorter:

Correction: your newborn does not wait four months for insurance

English-language sources still repeat that a foreign newborn must wait four months, or six, before joining National Health Insurance. This is out of date.

Since the November 2017 amendment to the National Health Insurance Act, a foreign newborn holding a residency document is enrolled from the day of birth, exactly as a Taiwanese newborn is. Confirmed

Work rights — and one gap nobody mentions

The Act of Gender Equality in Employment applies to all employees and does not restrict by nationality:

  • Maternity leave: 8 weeks. Fully paid if you've been employed six months or more; half pay if less.
  • Prenatal checkup leave: 7 days, paid.
  • Accompanying checkup and paternity leave: 7 days, paid. Employers refusing face fines up to NT$300,000.
  • Parental leave without pay: available after six months' service, until the child turns three. Not restricted by nationality.
The gap

Parental leave allowance — the payment, not the leave — is an Employment Insurance benefit. Under Article 5 of the Employment Insurance Act, it covers Taiwanese nationals and foreign nationals married to a household-registered Taiwanese citizen.

Which means: a foreign employee who is not married to a Taiwanese citizen can take parental leave, but will not receive the allowance. Unpaid leave, in practice.

Labour Insurance maternity benefit is different and has no nationality restriction — 280 days of coverage before delivery (181 for preterm) qualifies you for a lump sum of two months' average insured salary. The separate "labour maternity subsidy" introduced 1 January 2026 does restrict by nationality.

What we could not verify

In the spirit of the rest of this guide:

  • Uninsured caesarean costs — no official schedule found.
  • Live-in postpartum helper (月嫂) rates — no official statistics.
  • Whether a doula may accompany you into the delivery room — no central regulation found; it appears to be hospital-by-hospital. Ask early.
  • International medical centre pages at NTU, Taipei Veterans General, Far Eastern Memorial and Cathay General — all links failed.
  • English-language postpartum centres outside Taipei.
  • Social-media community evidence is thinner than we'd like; our search budget ran out before we could sweep Reddit and the larger Facebook groups properly.

If you take one thing from this

Decide, early, who will be in the room with you — someone who understands both the language and the system, and who has agreed in advance to speak on your behalf if you cannot.

That person might be your partner, a friend who's given birth here, a doula, or a nurse you've built a relationship with. It matters less who it is than that the decision was made before you were in labour.

Everything else in this guide is logistics. That part is the one that changes how the day actually feels.

Questions we get asked

Am I covered by NHI for pregnancy and birth?

It depends on how you hold residency, not how long you've been here. Employed: covered from your first day of work. Not employed: after six months of residence. Once insured, prenatal care and birth are covered, and childbirth is exempt from copayment under Article 48.

Does my newborn have to wait to get health insurance?

No. Since November 2017, a foreign newborn holding a residency document is enrolled from the day of birth. The "four month wait" you may have read is out of date.

Which hospitals really provide obstetric care in English?

Fewer than the lists suggest. Only one hospital's own website explicitly placed obstetrics inside its international medical services: China Medical University Hospital in Taichung. Everywhere else, call the international desk and ask specifically about obstetrics — and ask separately about prenatal visits, labour, and the postpartum ward.

Can I have a home birth or waterbirth?

Both exist but are rare. Midwife-attended births are under 1% of all births. We could verify only two places offering waterbirth. Most midwifery teams work in Mandarin. The government's gentle birth pilot programme that older articles cite ended over a decade ago.

Can a doula come into the delivery room with me?

We found no central regulation either way — it appears to be decided hospital by hospital, and most allow one birth companion. Ask this question early and get the answer in writing if you can, because it is not something you want to negotiate during labour.
